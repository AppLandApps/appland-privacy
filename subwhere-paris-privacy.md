# Paris métro station depths

Research of 11–14 October 2026 for the Subwhere Paris track profile. It covers:
- every published figure found on how deep the platforms of the 16 Métro lines lie;
- which line runs above which at the interchanges;
- the engineers' longitudinal profiles, now of lines 1 to 7, 7bis, 11 and parts of 12 and 13.

## Summary

**The engineers' profiles now set 168 station levels, up from 93 on 12 October.** On 12–13 October Stef fetched Le Génie civil's plates of April 1906 at full resolution, and L. Biette's two books on the Métro's construction (1922 and 1928):
- **A. Dumas, « Situation nouvelle des lignes projetées et état des travaux », *Le Génie civil*, 21 and 28 April 1906, Pl. XXVI–XXX:** the execution designs of every line then being built, with the rail level at every change of gradient:
  - line 4, Porte de Clignancourt to Porte d'Orléans;
  - line 5, Gare de l'Est to the Seine;
  - line 6, Place d'Italie to Nation;
  - line 7, Opéra to Louis Blanc, and today's 7bis round its terminal loop;
  - the Nord-Sud: today's line 12 from Montparnasse to Madeleine and from Saint-Lazare to Saint-Georges, and line 13 from Place de Clichy to Porte de Saint-Ouen.
- **L. Biette, *Les chemins de fer urbains parisiens* (1928):** single rail levels where the works were difficult:
  - line 7 under line 1's Louvre – Rivoli (18.39 m), at Châtelet (27.98 m) and in front of the Hôtel de Ville (22.04 m);
  - line 10 under the Seine at Mirabeau (14.13 m, then line 8).
- **His *Le Métropolitain de Paris* (1922):** line 9's lowest rail at Alma (20.03 m).

**How far the 1906 designs can be trusted as built.** They print the same levels as Hervieu's as-built plates of 1908 wherever both print one: line 4 over line 3 at Réaumur (28.78) and over line 6 at Denfert (55.60), line 5 at République (30.12) and Gare d'Austerlitz (41.39), line 7 at Opéra (25.23), and line 4's lowest rail under the Seine (16.05). Where the design is known to have changed, the plate is left out:
- Montmartre (line 12, Pigalle onward): the plan put Abbesses's platforms 22.6 m down; they were built about 30 m down.
- Line 5's Place d'Italie: 52.28 planned, 50.50 built (Hervieu's level is used).
- Line 13's Liège: the plan's station was at the rue de Londres, about 210 m south of where it was built.
- Les Halles (rebuilt 1977), line 13's Saint-Lazare (rebuilt 1975), and the terminal loop past Porte d'Orléans (not the 2013 extension's track).

**How the plates were read.** Every stretch was checked: the printed level at its end must equal the previous level plus gradient × length, within 2 cm. A few engraving slips were found that way and fixed by the arithmetic, for example line 4's Vaugirard block (15.5 ‰ printed where 18.5 ‰ fits, which the Nord-Sud plate confirms by printing line 4 at 33.22 there).

**Laying a profile on the app's route.** Each profile is pinned to the app's station points. Those are stop positions, often 30–150 m from the real platform centres, so the distance between two stations rarely matches the plate. The slopes now keep their printed lengths and the level stretches take up the difference, so the gradients stay as printed (at most 4.0 %). The one exception is line 12 between Saint-Lazare and Trinité (4.5 %), where the app's Saint-Lazare point lies about 150 m east of the platforms on the plan.

**Datum.** The 1906 plates and Biette's levels are on the same datum as the plates of 1901–1908 (they share levels), so all are taken 0.35 m lower to give NGF-IGN69.

**What the app now uses.** 212 of the 405 station levels come from published sources, up from 145 on 12 October:
- 168 on the engineers' profiles;
- 22 fixed to a published figure, 11 within a published range and 2 drawn towards less certain figures;
- 6 between two published levels (line 5, Stalingrad to Hoche);
- 3 described as deep with no figure, which follow their neighbours (Pont Neuf, Mirabeau, Mairie des Lilas).

The other 187 aim at a typical depth (6.5 m to the rails on the original lines): line 9 36, line 8 33, line 7 27 (south of Opéra and north of Louis Blanc), line 13 26, line 10 21, line 12 13, lines 3 and 14 9 each, line 1 8, others 5.

**Interchanges.** 66 relations are placed; 9 fall short of the 5 m minimum, all between two published levels, so the real gap there is smaller than the app's rule: Bastille (line 1 3.4 m over line 5), Place de Clichy (line 2 3.9 m over line 13; on the plate the crossing lies 120 m before the platform's centre, where line 13 is 2.7 m lower), Gare du Nord (line 4 4.1 m over line 5), Louis Blanc (7bis 4.3 m over line 7), and five of 4.5–4.9 m as before. Line 12 now passes at least 5 m under line 3 at the boulevard Haussmann, as the plate shows.

**Conflicts, and how they were settled:**
- **Line 12's Saint-Lazare.** The plan has two stations side by side there: the through line's at 24.04 (where line 13 starts) and the Montmartre line's at 19.90. Today's line 12 is the line to Montmartre, so 19.90.
- **Bercy viaduct.** Biette gives line 6's rail over the Seine at 42.85, the 1906 plate 42.25. Both say 14.95 m above the water, with water levels 0.60 m apart, so the plate's level is kept.
- **Cité, Gare d'Austerlitz, Opéra, line 1's plate, République:** as on 12 October (see the build notes).

**What's still missing.** No profile yet for lines 8, 9, 10, 3bis and 14, line 7 south of Opéra and north of Louis Blanc, line 12 north of Saint-Georges, line 13 south of Place de Clichy and its Les Courtilles branch, and the later extensions. Le Génie civil described line 8 (1913), line 9 (1922–23) and line 10 as they opened, so more of its last-of-tome plates could fill them in. RATP's answer to the records request, due by 30 October, would replace the estimates entirely.

**Caveats:**
- The 1906 plates are the execution designs, not as-built surveys. They agree with the as-built levels wherever both exist, but small changes during the works can't be ruled out elsewhere.
- The 0.35 m datum is a median: a single station's printed ground can differ from IGN's by up to a metre, more where the app's point sits on a slope.
- Some stations show their two directions up to a metre or more apart, because each direction's ground is sampled at a slightly different point. Fixing that is proposed but not done.
- Quotes read through the automated page reader may be cut to about 125 characters.

## The engineers' profiles

Where the engineers' longitudinal profiles survive, the app follows them, every gradient and level stretch: each station's level comes from the profile, and the track between from its gradients (the slopes keep their printed lengths; the level stretches take up the difference to the app's station spacing). The plates of 1901-1908 print the ground 0.35 m above IGN's (the median of 242 printed ground levels), so their levels are taken 0.35 m lower; the 1935 profile's ground agrees with IGN's, so its levels are taken as printed. Rails are in metres NGF-IGN69; depths are to the rails, from the ground the app uses (the direction named).
